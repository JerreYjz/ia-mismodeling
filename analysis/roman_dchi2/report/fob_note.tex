\noindent These notes follow the code (\texttt{v3/roman\_test.py},
function \texttt{fit}; the linear version in \texttt{v4/split\_pm.py} and
\texttt{v5/linear\_cov.py}) step by step. Section~\ref{sec:summary} is the
short version.

\section{Ingredients}

\paragraph{Data vector.} Real-space cosmic shear, $\xi_\pm^{ij}(\theta)$ for
the 36 source-bin pairs and 15 angular bins, $N = 1080$ elements. The
\emph{contaminated} (``truth'') data vector is noiseless:
\begin{equation}
\vd = \vm_{\rm GG}(\vp_{\rm fid}) + \bm{\xi}_{\rm IA}^{\rm FLAMINGO},
\end{equation}
where $\vm_{\rm GG}$ is the lensing-only prediction at the fiducial cosmology
(D3A) and $\bm{\xi}_{\rm IA}^{\rm FLAMINGO}$ is the GI $+$ II contribution
computed by cosmolike from the FLAMINGO $P_{\delta E}(k,z)$ and $P_{EE}(k,z)$
(converted to the cosmolike convention, injected through the
\texttt{sim\_IA} tables).

\paragraph{Mask.} A boolean vector selects $n \le N$ elements (the scale
cut). Everything below uses only the kept elements; I drop the mask from the
notation.

\paragraph{Covariance.} $C$ ($n\times n$), the data covariance on the mask:
either the Gaussian $\xi_\pm$ covariance of these notes or the production
\texttt{example1\_cov} ($\xi_\pm$ block). $C$ is held fixed (it does not
depend on the parameters).

\paragraph{Model and parameters.} $\vm(\vp)$ is cosmolike's prediction with
the analysis IA model. The parameter vector is
\begin{equation}
\vp = (\underbrace{\Omega_m,\ A_s\ [,\ n_s,\ H_0,\ \Omega_b]}_{\vp_c\ \text{cosmology}},\ \underbrace{A_1,\ \eta_1\ [,\ A_2,\ \eta_2,\ b_{\rm TA}]}_{\vp_{\rm IA}}),
\end{equation}
with $A_i(z) = A_i\,[(1+z)/1.62]^{\eta_i}$. \emph{Case A} frees $\Omega_m$
and $A_s$ only; \emph{case B} also frees $n_s$, $H_0$, $\Omega_b$. NLA has
two IA parameters, TATT five.

\paragraph{Priors.} IA parameters are bounded to the chain's flat priors
($A_1, \eta_1, A_2, \eta_2 \in [-5, 5]$, $b_{\rm TA} \in [0, 2]$). In case B
the extra cosmological parameters get Gaussian priors centred on the fiducial
with the width of the chain's flat prior (see Eq.~\ref{eq:flatvar}):
$\sigma_{n_s} = 0.2/\sqrt{12}$, $\sigma_{H_0} = 36/\sqrt{12}$,
$\sigma_{\Omega_b} = 0.04/\sqrt{12}$.

\section{Step 1: the best fit (MAP)}

The best fit minimises
\begin{equation}
\chi^2(\vp) = [\vd - \vm(\vp)]\tp C^{-1}[\vd - \vm(\vp)]
\;+\; \sum_{a\,\in\,\text{Gaussian priors}}\frac{(p_a - p_a^{\rm fid})^2}{\sigma_a^2},
\label{eq:chi2}
\end{equation}
subject to the IA bounds. Because the data are noiseless and the prior is
flat in the IA parameters, the minimiser is the maximum of the posterior
(MAP). If the analysis model were the truth, the minimum would be at
$\vp_{\rm fid}$ with $\chi^2 = 0$; any offset is caused by the model
mismatch.

\paragraph{How it is minimised.} Each cosmolike call is expensive when the
cosmology changes (CAMB), cheap when only IA parameters change. So:
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=1pt]
\item Fit the IA parameters alone at the fiducial cosmology, from several
  starting points (both TATT branches), keeping the lowest $\chi^2$.
\item Linearise the model in the cosmological parameters around the
  fiducial, at the current IA solution:
  \begin{equation}
  \vm(\vp_c, \vp_{\rm IA}) \simeq \vm(\vp_c^{\rm fid}, \vp_{\rm IA}) + J_c(\vp_{\rm IA})\,(\vp_c - \vp_c^{\rm fid}),
  \qquad (J_c)_{ia} = \frac{\partial m_i}{\partial p_{c,a}},
  \label{eq:lin}
  \end{equation}
  with $J_c$ from central finite differences (steps $\Delta\Omega_m = 0.01$,
  $\Delta A_s = 0.05\times10^{-9}$, \dots).
\item Minimise Eq.~(\ref{eq:chi2}) over all parameters with this model (IA
  part exact, cosmology part linear), by trust-region least squares on the
  whitened residual $L^{-1}(\vd - \vm)$, $C = LL\tp$.
\item Recompute $J_c$ at the new IA solution and repeat once.
\end{enumerate}
The final $\chi^2$ is evaluated with the exact model at the solution. This
was checked against a fully non-linear fit (cosmology also exact): identical
to all quoted digits. The cosmological shifts are a few tenths of $\sigma$,
well inside the linear regime.

\section{Step 2: the parameter shift}

The quantities of interest are $\vth = (\Omega_m, S_8)$, with
$S_8 = \sigma_8\sqrt{\Omega_m/0.3}$ computed by CAMB. The shift is
\begin{equation}
\delta\vth = \big(\Omega_m^{\rm MAP} - \Omega_m^{\rm fid},\ \ S_8^{\rm MAP} - S_8^{\rm fid}\big).
\end{equation}

\section{Step 3: the uncertainty of \texorpdfstring{$\vth$}{(Omega_m, S8)}}

\paragraph{Fisher matrix.} At the best fit, with $J = \partial\vm/\partial\vp$
($n \times n_p$; cosmology columns $J_c$ as above, IA columns by central
differences with step 0.05),
\begin{equation}
F = J\tp C^{-1} J + P^{-1},
\qquad \mathrm{Cov}(\vp) = F^{-1}.
\label{eq:fisher}
\end{equation}
$P^{-1}$ is diagonal: the case-B Gaussian priors on $n_s, H_0, \Omega_b$,
zero for $\Omega_m$ and $A_s$, and for each IA parameter the variance of its
flat prior,
\begin{equation}
p \sim \mathcal{U}[a, b] \;\Rightarrow\; \mathrm{Var}(p) = \frac{(b-a)^2}{12},
\qquad \sigma_{A_1} = \sigma_{\eta_1} = \sigma_{A_2} = \sigma_{\eta_2} = \frac{10}{\sqrt{12}} = 2.89,\quad
\sigma_{b_{\rm TA}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{12}} = 0.58 .
\label{eq:flatvar}
\end{equation}
A Fisher matrix cannot represent a flat prior; replacing it by a Gaussian of
the same variance is the standard approximation. It matters only for
parameters the data barely constrain (for TATT, $\eta_2$ and $b_{\rm TA}$).

\paragraph{Marginalising and projecting.} Inverting $F$ marginalises over all
other parameters (IA included). The covariance of $\vth$ follows from the
Jacobian $G = \partial\vth/\partial\vp$ ($2 \times n_p$):
\begin{equation}
\Sigma_\theta = G\,F^{-1}\,G\tp,\qquad
G = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \partial S_8/\partial\Omega_m & \partial S_8/\partial A_s & \cdots & 0 \end{pmatrix},
\end{equation}
with the $S_8$ derivatives by finite differences through CAMB. The IA columns
of $G$ are zero ($\vth$ depends on cosmology only).

\section{Step 4: the figure of bias}

\begin{equation}
\boxed{\ \fob = \sqrt{\delta\vth\tp\,\Sigma_\theta^{-1}\,\delta\vth}\ }
\label{eq:fob}
\end{equation}
This is the Mahalanobis distance between the biased and the true
$(\Omega_m, S_8)$, in units of the marginalised Fisher error ellipse.
Writing $\Sigma_\theta$ with $\sigma_{\Omega_m}$, $\sigma_{S_8}$ and
correlation $\rho$, and $x = \delta\Omega_m/\sigma_{\Omega_m}$,
$y = \delta S_8/\sigma_{S_8}$:
\begin{equation}
\fob^2 = \frac{x^2 - 2\rho\,xy + y^2}{1-\rho^2}.
\end{equation}
So the FoB is not $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$: a shift along the degeneracy direction
(sign of $xy$ equal to the sign of $\rho$) counts less, a shift across it
counts more. For $\rho = 0$ it reduces to $\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$.

\paragraph{Example} (Gaussian covariance, $K_0 = 0.3/D(z)$, NLA, case A).
$\delta\Omega_m/\sigma = +0.07$, $\delta S_8/\sigma = +0.31$,
$\sigma(S_8) = 0.0048$, $\fob = 0.31$: almost all of the bias is in $S_8$,
and $|\delta S_8| = 0.31\times0.0048 = 1.5\times10^{-3}$.

\paragraph{How to read ``$0.3\sigma$''.} For a Gaussian in two dimensions the
68.3\% contour is at $\fob = \sqrt{2.30} = 1.52$ (not at 1), and the 95.4\%
contour at $\sqrt{6.18} = 2.49$. A shift with $\fob = 0.3$ therefore moves
the centre by $0.3/1.52 \approx 0.2$ of the 68\% contour's ``radius'' along
that direction. The DES Y3 convention ``$0.3\sigma_{\rm 2D}$'' is defined on
the marginalised posterior contours; depending on which convention is
matched, numbers can differ by a factor of order 1.5 to 2. The paper should
state Eq.~(\ref{eq:fob}) explicitly.

\section{Step 5: the uncertainty of the FoB from FLAMINGO's finite volume}

The injected spectra carry the simulation's measurement error. Let
$\bm{\epsilon}$ be the vector of FLAMINGO errors per ``cell'' (snapshot $s$,
spectrum $X \in \{\delta E, EE\}$, $k$ node $i$), and
$\mathsf{J}_{\rm cell} = \partial\bm{\xi}/\partial P_X(k_i, z_s)$ the cell
Jacobian (one \texttt{sim\_IA} table per cell). A noise realisation
$\delta P = \bm{\epsilon}\odot\bm{u}$, $\bm{u} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)$, changes
the data by $\delta\vd = \mathsf{J}_{\rm cell}\,(\bm{\epsilon}\odot\bm{u})$ and the
shift by (linear estimator, Sec.~\ref{sec:linear})
\begin{equation}
\delta(\delta\vth) = G F^{-1} J\tp C^{-1}\,\delta\vd \equiv \mathsf{B}\,\delta\vd ,
\qquad
V_n = \mathsf{B}\,\mathsf{J}_{\rm cell} D_\epsilon^2 \mathsf{J}_{\rm cell}\tp\,\mathsf{B}\tp .
\end{equation}
The FoB changes along its gradient $\bm{g} = \partial\fob/\partial\delta\vth
= \Sigma_\theta^{-1}\delta\vth/\fob$, so
\begin{equation}
\sigma_{\fob} = \sqrt{\bm{g}\tp V_n\,\bm{g}} .
\end{equation}
This is the ``$\pm$'' quoted with the FoB. It is a linearised estimate and
becomes unreliable when the FoB is close to zero (the FoB cannot be
negative).

\section{The linear-response shortcut}\label{sec:linear}

For quick scans over many masks the fit can be replaced by its linearisation
around a reference point $\vp_0$ (fiducial cosmology, IA at a representative
best fit). With $\vr = \vd - \vm(\vp_0)$ and $J$, $F$ evaluated at $\vp_0$,
one Gauss-Newton step of Eq.~(\ref{eq:chi2}) gives
\begin{equation}
\delta\vp = F^{-1}J\tp C^{-1}\vr,\qquad \delta\vth = G\,\delta\vp,
\label{eq:linshift}
\end{equation}
(the prior terms are centred on $\vp_0$, which for the cosmology is the
fiducial). The $\chi^2$ left at the best fit is the part of the residual the
model cannot absorb,
\begin{equation}
\Delta\chi^2_{\rm min} = \vr_\perp\tp C^{-1}\vr_\perp,\qquad
\vr_\perp = \vr - J\,\delta\vp .
\end{equation}
The same estimator gives the bias from any extra contamination
$\delta\vd$ (for example the white II term alone: $\delta\vth = G F^{-1}J\tp
C^{-1}\delta\vd$), and adding a nuisance parameter (the white-term amplitude)
is one more column in $J$ and one more prior entry in $P^{-1}$. On the masks
where both exist the linear FoB agrees with the full fit to $\pm0.04$,
except for TATT fits that move far from $\vp_0$ (prior-bound solutions),
where the full fit is the number to quote.

\section{Controls}
Two checks run at every mask:
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Matched model}: TATT fitted to a data vector generated by TATT
  gives $\fob = 0.000$. This confirms the minimiser and the Fisher
  machinery: with the right model the MAP sits exactly at the truth.
\item \emph{Injection path}: NLA fitted to an NLA data vector built through
  the same \texttt{sim\_IA} tables as FLAMINGO gives $\fob < 0.01$. This
  confirms that the table injection itself adds no bias.
\end{itemize}
Hence any nonzero FoB is model mismatch.

\section{MAP versus posterior mean}
The FoB above is a MAP statistic. A chain reports marginalised posterior
means, which also move when a weakly constrained parameter has an asymmetric
prior volume (projection effect). The MCMC at $K_0 = 0.3/D(z)$ reproduced the
MAP shifts, and showed a $0.24\sigma$ shift of the TATT posterior mean even
on a TATT truth (from $\eta_2$ and $b_{\rm TA}$). For chains, quote MAP
shifts, or posterior-mean shifts minus those of the matched-model chain.

\section{Summary}\label{sec:summary}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=2pt]
\item Truth: $\vd = \vm_{\rm GG}(\vp_{\rm fid}) + \bm{\xi}_{\rm IA}^{\rm FLAMINGO}$, noiseless, masked.
\item Fit the analysis model with all parameters free (IA exact, cosmology
  linearised and refreshed), minimising $\chi^2$ with the priors:
  $\vp^{\rm MAP}$.
\item $\delta\vth = \vth(\vp^{\rm MAP}) - \vth(\vp_{\rm fid})$, $\vth = (\Omega_m, S_8)$.
\item $F = J\tp C^{-1}J + P^{-1}$ at $\vp^{\rm MAP}$ (flat IA priors as
  Gaussians of variance $\text{range}^2/12$); $\Sigma_\theta = G F^{-1} G\tp$.
\item $\fob = \sqrt{\delta\vth\tp\Sigma_\theta^{-1}\delta\vth}$; uncertainty
  from FLAMINGO's errors through the cell Jacobian.
\item Shortcut: $\delta\vth \simeq G F^{-1}J\tp C^{-1}[\vd - \vm(\vp_0)]$.
\end{enumerate}
