\section*{Summary}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{The question.} The Roman 3x2pt figure of bias (FoB) on the
FLAMINGO CP truth is 1.5 to 3 times that of cosmic shear for every IA
model, the exact IA shape included: with NLA it crosses 0.3 at
$K_0=0.3\hMpc$ and reaches $\refresh{\fobTxNlaEight}$ at 0.8. Is that an IA
problem, and can it be fixed?

\paragraph{The answer.} It is the IA amplitude's redshift evolution, and a
one-parameter extension fixes it. The truth's IA amplitude is not a power
law in $1+z$: $\ln A$ is convex in $\ln(1+z)$, with a slope of
$\refresh{\slopeLow}$ from $z=0.2$ to 1 and $\refresh{\slopeHigh}$ from 1
to 4. A single power law ($\eta_1=\refresh{\etaPl}$) splits the difference
and sits up to $\refresh{\plDevMid}$ percent below the truth at $z=0.2$ to 0.5,
where the low-redshift lens bins of $\gamma_t$ sit, and $\refresh{\plDevHigh}$ percent below at
$z=3$ to 4. $\gamma_t$ and $w$ turn that into an $\Om$ shift. A curved power law,
$A(z)=A_1x^{\eta_1+\eta_2\ln x}$ with $x=(1+z)/1.62$, brings NLA in 3x2pt
from $\refresh{\fobTxNlaSix}$ to $\refresh{\fobTxQSix}$ at $K_0=0.6$ and from
$\refresh{\fobTxNlaEight}$ to $\refresh{\fobTxQEight}$ at 0.8. With the HMk
shape of the halo-model note the FoB stays at or below
$\refresh{\fobTxHmkQMax}$ in 3x2pt and $\refresh{\fobCsHmkQMax}$ in cosmic
shear for every $K_0$ to 0.8. The extra parameter widens $\sigma(S_8)$ and
$\sigma(\Om)$ by at most $\refresh{\costTxQSMax}$ and
$\refresh{\costTxQOMax}$ percent.

\paragraph{How we know.} Five tests (Section~\ref{sec:diag}). (1) Every IA
model shifts $\Om$ by about the same amount in 3x2pt, so the cause is
common to all shapes. (2) The fit and truth pipelines agree on the native
IA to $\chi^2=\refresh{\checkChiTx}$. (3) With the truth's own shape and a
free amplitude at every snapshot the FoB is at most
$\refresh{\fobTxOracleMax}$: the shape carries no bias, the amplitude law
carries all of it. (4) One amplitude per source bin, the usual freer
model, makes 3x2pt worse (up to $\refresh{\fobTxNlazMax}$), because
$\gamma_t$ sees the low-redshift tail of each source bin and $\xi_\pm$ the
whole bin; the amplitude must be a function of $z$, not of the bin. (5)
Freedom at $z\le0.2$ alone, where the sample's stellar-mass window makes
the truth's amplitude dip, does not help (up to
$\refresh{\fobTxCtrlLzMax}$ with the truth's shape); the curvature over
$z=0.2$ to 4 is what matters.

\paragraph{Limits.} One simulation, one sample definition: how strongly a
real Roman sample's IA amplitude departs from a power law depends on its
own selection. The Fisher shifts are linear; minimizer checks agree
(Section~\ref{sec:results}). The A(z) corrections are held at the
fiducial cosmology, as all the templates of these notes.

\section{Fiducial setup}
% =====================================================================

The setup of the halo-model note: Roman $\xi_\pm$ with the cut
$k_{\rm UV}=K_0/D(z)$; 3x2pt adds $\gamma_t$ of sources behind lenses at
$K_0=0.5$ and $w$ at 0.2, eight lens bins sharing the source $n(z)$ with
free linear biases; CMB prior; every parameter free; the CP truth
(stitched FLAMINGO with the close-pair II term, 13 snapshots $z=0$ to 4);
the data vector is the model's own $\Lambda$CDM vector plus the truth's
IA, so a bias can only come from the IA modelling. FoB is the 2D Fisher
shift of $(\Om, S_8)$ in units of the marginalized errors, against 0.3.

Every redshift-evolution model below multiplies the power-law amplitude
by a function $g(z)$,
\begin{equation}
A(z)=A_1\,x^{\eta_1}\,g(z),\qquad x=\frac{1+z}{1.62},
\label{eq:g}
\end{equation}
applied exactly through the per-snapshot IA templates (GI linear, II
quadratic in $A$; $\gamma_t$ rows scaled with the lens bias). The
templates are piecewise linear in $z$ between the 13 snapshots.

\section{Diagnosis}
\label{sec:diag}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{1. The bias is common to all IA shapes.} In 3x2pt at $K_0=0.8$
NLA shifts $\Om$ by $\refresh{\muOmTxNlaEight}\,\sigma$, TATT by
$\refresh{\muOmTxTattEight}$, HMk by $\refresh{\muOmTxHmkEight}$ and the
truth's own shape (the control) by $\refresh{\muOmTxCtrlEight}$. In cosmic
shear the control moves $\Om$ by $\refresh{\shiftOmCs}$; in 3x2pt by
$\refresh{\shiftOmTx}$, while $\sigma(\Om)$ shrinks only from
$\refresh{\sigOmCs}$ to $\refresh{\sigOmTx}$. 3x2pt is not just more
precise: the same IA error moves $\Om$ more in it.

\paragraph{2. The pipelines agree.} The truth's IA is built by a separate
driver from the FLAMINGO spectra per snapshot, the fit uses cosmolike's
native NLA. Summed over snapshots, the truth driver's NLA templates
reproduce the native NLA to $\chi^2=\refresh{\checkChiCs}$ (cosmic shear)
and $\refresh{\checkChiTx}$ (3x2pt) at $A_1=3.5$, $\eta_1=0.7$. $\gamma_t$
and $\xi_\pm$ share the same GI, $b_i P_{\delta I}$ at the lens redshifts
and $P_{\delta I}$ in the lensing kernels.

\paragraph{3. The amplitude law carries all of it.} The control template
reproduces the truth exactly at the truth's per-snapshot amplitudes. With
that shape and a free $g$ at every snapshot (the oracle, 13 amplitudes),
the FoB is at most $\refresh{\fobTxOracleMax}$ in 3x2pt and
$\refresh{\fobCsOracleMax}$ in cosmic shear over every $K_0$; with $g$ on
four free nodes it is at most $\refresh{\fobTxCtrlNdMax}$ and
$\refresh{\fobCsCtrlNdMax}$. With the power law it is
$\refresh{\fobTxCtrlEight}$ in 3x2pt at $K_0=0.8$. Nothing in the IA
shape biases 3x2pt; the power law in $1+z$ does.

\paragraph{4. The amplitude must depend on $z$, not on the bin.} The usual
freer model, one $A_1$ per source bin, fits $\gamma_t$ and $w$ alone
perfectly (2x2pt FoB at most $\refresh{\fobTwoNlazMax}$, against
$\refresh{\fobTwoNlaMax}$ for NLA) but gives up to
$\refresh{\fobTxNlazMax}$ in 3x2pt. The GI of $\gamma_t$ comes from the part
of each source bin that overlaps the lenses, its low-redshift tail; the GI
and II of $\xi_\pm$ come from the whole bin. With an amplitude that rises
with $z$, the two want different constants for the same bin, and the
compromise leaks into $\Om$. A continuous $A(z)$ serves both.

\paragraph{5. Which part of the amplitude law fails.} The truth's
amplitude has two departures from a power law (Fig.~\ref{fig:amp}). (a)
A dip at $z\le0.1$: the FLAMINGO IA sample is
$10<\log_{10}M_*/M_\odot<\log M_{\rm max}(z)$, the upper edge the 97.5th
percentile of the Roman-like target at each $z$, which is
$\refresh{\logMmaxZero}$ at $z=0$, $\refresh{\logMmaxOne}$ at 0.1 and
$\refresh{\logMmaxTwo}$ at 0.2; the sample's mean $\log M_*$ is
$\refresh{\logMmeanZero}$ at $z=0$ against $\refresh{\logMmeanMid}$ at
$z=0.2$ to 1, and its amplitude drops with it. (b) A curvature: the local
slope $d\ln A/d\ln(1+z)$ rises from about 0.7 at $z<1$ to about 1 above
(Fig.~\ref{fig:amp}, bottom). Only (b) matters. With the truth's own shape
and free tents at $z=0$ and 0.2, the FoB is $\refresh{\fobTxCtrlLzThree}$
in 3x2pt at $K_0=0.3$ against $\refresh{\fobTxCtrlThree}$ for the power
law: the Roman sources and lenses at $z\le0.1$ carry too little weight. The
fits of Fig.~\ref{fig:amp} show the same: the curved power law matches the
truth within 5 percent from $z=0.2$ to 3 and is free to miss $z\le0.1$
by 50 percent. What sets the curvature in FLAMINGO (the evolution of the
host-halo masses at fixed stellar mass, the central fraction, the upper
mass edge rising to $\log M_*=11.35$ at $z=3$) has not been separated.

\section{Strategies}
% =====================================================================

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{llc}
\toprule
name & $g(z)$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:g} & extra parameters \\
\midrule
curved power law & $x^{\eta_2\ln x}$ & 1 \\
mass-informed & $\langle(M_*/10^{10.5})^\beta\rangle_z/\langle\cdot\rangle_{z=0.6}$ over the sample & 1 \\
mass-informed + curvature & product of the two & 2 \\
low and high tents & $1+\sum c_iT_i(z)$, tents at $z=0$, 0.2, 2, 3 & 4 \\
nodes & piecewise linear, free at $z=0$, 0.2, 0.5, 2; 1 at $z=1$, 4 & 4 \\
per snapshot & free at every snapshot but $z=1$, 4 & 11 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The curved power law is the smallest change that bends $\ln A$ in
$\ln(1+z)$. The mass-informed form uses what a survey measures, its
sample's stellar-mass distribution per redshift, with an amplitude-mass
slope $\beta$; it was built for the low-redshift dip before test 5 showed
the dip does not matter. Nodes and the per-snapshot form are the flexible
bounds. Each is run on several IA shapes: native NLA, TATT, the HMk
template of the halo-model note, and the truth's own shape.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
% =====================================================================

Figure~\ref{fig:fob} shows the FoB against $K_0$. In 3x2pt:
\begin{itemize}
\item NLA with the curved power law gives at most
$\refresh{\fobTxQMaxSix}$ to $K_0=0.6$ and $\refresh{\fobTxQEight}$ at 0.8
(NLA: $\refresh{\fobTxNlaSix}$ and $\refresh{\fobTxNlaEight}$). Nodes give
$\refresh{\fobTxNdMaxSix}$ and $\refresh{\fobTxNdEight}$, one amplitude per
snapshot $\refresh{\fobTxSnMaxSix}$ and $\refresh{\fobTxSnEight}$: one
parameter does as well as eleven.
\item The mass-informed form helps less ($\refresh{\fobTxMMaxSix}$ to 0.6,
$\refresh{\fobTxMEight}$ at 0.8) and fails its physical check: its
slope comes out $\beta=\refresh{\betaCtrl}$ (with the truth's shape), i.e.\
more massive galaxies less aligned, the opposite of what is observed. It
bends $A(z)$ at low redshift and stands in for the curvature, poorly. With
the curvature added it reaches $\refresh{\fobTxMqMaxSix}$ to 0.6, no
better than the curvature alone.
\item HMk with the curved power law gives at most
$\refresh{\fobTxHmkQMax}$ at every $K_0$ to 0.8 ($\refresh{\fobTxHmkQEight}$
at 0.8), HMk with nodes $\refresh{\fobTxHmkNdMax}$. With the A(z) fixed,
the better shape pays off at high $K_0$, where NLA's shape error grows.
\item TATT with nodes gives $\refresh{\fobTxTattNdEight}$ at 0.8.
\end{itemize}
In cosmic shear the same strategies also help: at $K_0=0.8$ NLA goes from
$\refresh{\fobCsNlaEight}$ to $\refresh{\fobCsQEight}$, HMk from
$\refresh{\fobCsHmkEight}$ to $\refresh{\fobCsHmkQEight}$, and the control
from $\refresh{\fobCsCtrlEight}$ to $\refresh{\fobCsCtrlNdEight}$ with
nodes: the residual cosmic-shear bias of the control was the amplitude
law too.

\paragraph{Cost.} The curved power law widens $\sigma(S_8)$ in 3x2pt by
$\refresh{\costTxQS}$ percent and $\sigma(\Om)$ by $\refresh{\costTxQO}$
percent at $K_0=0.6$ (at most $\refresh{\costTxQSMax}$ percent for any
$K_0$); nodes by $\refresh{\costTxNdS}$ and $\refresh{\costTxNdO}$; one
amplitude per snapshot by $\refresh{\costTxSnS}$ and $\refresh{\costTxSnO}$
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cost}).

\paragraph{Minimizer check.} The full minimizer (no linear approximation)
gives, in 3x2pt, $\refresh{\minFobQSix}$ and $\refresh{\minFobQEight}$ for
NLA with the curved power law at $K_0=0.6$ and 0.8,
$\refresh{\minFobHmkQSix}$ and $\refresh{\minFobHmkQEight}$ for HMk with it,
and $\refresh{\minFobNlaEight}$ for NLA at 0.8; in cosmic shear
$\refresh{\minFobCsHmkQEight}$ for HMk with it at 0.8.

\section{Recommendation}
% =====================================================================

Fit the IA amplitude with at least a curved power law in 3x2pt, or a
continuous $A(z)$ with nodes; never one amplitude per source bin when
$\gamma_t$ is in the data vector. With the HMk shape and the curved power
law, this simulation supports $K_0=0.8$ in both probes.

\section{Numerical checks and code}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Template interpolation.} The snapshot templates reproduce the
native NLA to $\chi^2\approx0.01$ for $\eta_1\le0.7$, but miss it by
$\chi^2\approx40$ at $\eta_1=2$, where $A(z)$ changes too fast for linear
interpolation between snapshots (check\_hm\_CP.log). The curved power law
fitted with the truth's shape has $g$ within $\refresh{\gQmax}$ percent of 1
at $z\le2$, a gentle bend the interpolation follows.

\paragraph{Where things are.} The runs:

\section{Open issues}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Origin of the curvature.} The convex $\ln A(\ln(1+z))$ of the
FLAMINGO sample is measured, not explained; host-halo mass at fixed
$M_*$, the central fraction and the rising upper mass edge are candidates.
A physical model of it (for instance the HMk amplitude $A_{
m eff}(z)$ from the
HOD and the halo-shape bias, which the halo-model note predicts per
snapshot) would replace the free $\eta_2$.

\paragraph{Mass-informed form.} With $\beta<0$ it is unphysical; a slope
per central and satellite, or the host-halo mass in place of $M_*$, has
not been tried.

\paragraph{Other samples.} How far a sample's amplitude departs from a
power law depends on its selection. The curved power law has not been
tested on other selections or on TNG300.
